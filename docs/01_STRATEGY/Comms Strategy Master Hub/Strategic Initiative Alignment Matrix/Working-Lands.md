# Working Lands

**Strategic Goal:** Protect Door County's farms, forests, and open spaces through acquisition or conservation easement. Advance watershed health, habitat connectivity, scenic character, and local food systems as essential parts of Door County's identity.

---

## What "Working Lands" Means

| Protection Method | Ownership | Land Use | Example |
|-------------------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Conservation Easement | Stays with landowner | Continues as farm/forest | Family farm protected from subdivision |
| Acquisition + Managed Use | DCLT owns | Responsible agriculture continues | Hay fields, managed timber |
| Acquisition + Restoration | DCLT owns | Agriculture phased out | Farmland → prairie over 5-10 years |

**Strategic Plan Target:**
> "25% of working lands acquired have management plans that include restoration to native habitat"

## Why Working Lands Matter (Draft Content)

Working lands—farms, managed forests, hayfields—aren't just scenic. They're infrastructure:

- **Water quality:** Well-managed fields filter runoff before it reaches the bay
- **Habitat corridors:** Field edges and woodlots connect fragmented ecosystems
- **Local food:** Door County's orchards, farms, and pastures feed the community
- **Character:** The patchwork of field and forest *is* Door County's identity
- **Climate resilience:** Healthy soils store carbon; diverse landscapes adapt better

When working lands get subdivided, all of this fragments. Protection keeps the landscape whole.
