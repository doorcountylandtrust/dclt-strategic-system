## Why Landowners Protect Their Land

Some people protect their land because they've walked it since childhood and can't imagine it becoming something else. Others do it to honor parents or grandparents who cleared the fields, built the barn, planted the orchard.

Some are thinking about taxes and estate planning. Some just want to know—really know—that the woods their kids played in will still be woods when their grandchildren are grown.

There's no single reason. But the instinct is the same: *this place matters, and I want it to stay this way.*

Since 1986, the Door County Land Trust has helped landowners do exactly that. Not by taking over. Not by telling you what to do. But by offering tools, expertise, and a promise: if you choose to protect your land, we'll honor that choice—permanently.

**You stay in control. You keep ownership. And your land stays protected, no matter who owns it next.**
[/block:text]

### Conservation Easements
**Keep your land. Protect it permanently.**

A conservation easement is a legal agreement that limits future development on your property—while you retain ownership. You can still live on the land, farm it, sell it, or pass it to your heirs. The easement simply ensures that certain protections stay in place, no matter who owns it next.

This is the most common tool we use. It's flexible, customized to your goals, and can offer significant tax benefits.

**Good for:** Landowners who want to stay on their land (or keep it in the family) while ensuring it's never subdivided or overdeveloped.
[cta: Learn More About Easements → #easements]

### Land Donations
**Give your land a future.**

Donating land to the Land Trust is one of the most powerful legacies a person can leave. We accept donations of ecologically significant properties and manage them as nature preserves—open to the public, protected forever.

Donations can qualify for substantial tax deductions and relieve you of ongoing property tax and management responsibilities.

**Good for:** Landowners without heirs who want the property protected, those with highly appreciated land, or those who want to see their land become a public treasure.
[cta: Learn More About Donations → #donations]

### Bargain Sales
**Sell your land for conservation—on your terms.**

A bargain sale means selling your property to the Land Trust for less than its appraised value. You receive cash, avoid some capital gains taxes, and may qualify for a charitable deduction on the difference. It makes conservation affordable for us and financially beneficial for you.

We purchase land selectively—properties with outstanding ecological or scenic value.

**Good for:** Landowners who need liquidity but want to ensure their land is protected, not developed.
[cta: Learn More About Sales → #sales]
[/block:feature-grid]

title: Conservation Easements — The Details

## What Is a Conservation Easement?

When you own land, you hold many rights: the right to build, subdivide, clear timber, install structures, and more. A conservation easement is a voluntary legal agreement in which you permanently give up *some* of those rights—typically development rights—in order to protect the land's natural, scenic, or agricultural character.

The easement is held by the Door County Land Trust. We monitor the property annually and ensure that all future owners honor the agreement. But **you keep the land.** You can live on it, farm it, sell it, pass it to your children. The restrictions simply travel with the deed, forever.

## What Can an Easement Protect?

- Farms and working agricultural land
- Forests and woodlands
- Wetlands, streams, and lakeshores
- Scenic views and open space
- Wildlife habitat and corridors

## How Flexible Are They?

Very. No two easements are alike. We write each one to reflect *your* goals and *your* property. You decide—with our guidance—what activities to allow and what to restrict. Want to reserve a building envelope for a future home? We can do that. Want to allow sustainable forestry? That's negotiable too.

## What Are the Benefits?

- **You keep ownership** — live on the land, sell it, pass it to heirs
- **Permanent protection** — the easement stays with the land forever, through all future owners
- **Potential tax benefits** — federal income tax deduction, possible estate tax reduction, potential property tax benefits (varies by situation)
- **Family clarity** — a clear "framework" can reduce disagreements when land passes to the next generation
- **No cost to you** — the Land Trust handles drafting, recording, and ongoing stewardship
- **Peace of mind** — you'll know your land will never become a subdivision or strip mall

## Can I Still Leave the Land to My Kids?

Yes. Your heirs inherit the land just like any other property. The easement simply means they (and all future owners) must follow the same restrictions you agreed to.

title: Land Donations — The Details

## Why Donate Land?

Donating land to the Door County Land Trust is one of the greatest conservation legacies a person or family can leave. Your property becomes a nature preserve—protected forever, open to the public, and cared for by our stewardship team.

## Who Is This Right For?

Land donation may be especially attractive if you:

- Want your land protected in its natural state but no longer wish to manage it
- Don't have heirs—or have heirs who can't or won't protect the property
- Own land you no longer use
- Could benefit from a substantial federal income tax deduction
- Own highly appreciated property (donation avoids capital gains tax you'd owe on a sale)
- Want to reduce estate tax burdens
- Would like to be relieved of property tax and management responsibilities

## What Happens to My Land?

We manage donated properties as nature preserves. Some are open for public hiking; others are managed for wildlife habitat or ecological restoration. We'll work with you to understand your wishes and honor them in our management plans.

## Can I Keep Living on Part of It?

Yes. You can donate a portion of your property while retaining ownership of the rest. Or you can donate the land but reserve a "life estate"—the right to live there for the rest of your life.

## How Does the Process Work?

It's simpler than you might think. We handle title work, surveys, and documentation. We guide you through the process step by step.

## What Is a Bargain Sale?

A bargain sale means selling your property to the Land Trust for less than its fair market value. The difference between the appraised value and the sale price counts as a charitable donation—which may entitle you to a tax deduction.

**Example:** If your land appraises for $500,000 and you sell it to us for $350,000, the $150,000 difference may qualify as a charitable contribution.

## What Are the Benefits?

- **Cash** — you receive payment for your land
- **Reduced capital gains tax** — you're taxed only on the sale portion, not the full value
- **Charitable deduction** — the "gift" portion may be tax-deductible
- **Conservation outcome** — your land is protected, not developed

## Does the Land Trust Buy All Properties?

No. We purchase land selectively—properties with significant ecological value, outstanding scenic views, or strategic importance for connecting protected areas. If your property qualifies, we'll discuss options.

## How Do I Find Out If My Land Qualifies?

Fill out our Landowner Inquiry Form. Our staff will review your property and reach out to discuss whether a sale or bargain sale might be a fit.

## What Happens When You Reach Out?

**It starts with a conversation.** No paperwork, no pressure. Just a chance to talk about your land, your goals, and what matters to you.

Here's how it typically works:

1. **You contact us.** Phone, email, or inquiry form—whatever's comfortable. Tell us a little about your property and what's on your mind.

2. **We talk.** One of our staff will ask questions and listen. We'll learn about your land, your family situation, and what you're hoping to accomplish.

3. **We visit (if you'd like).** For landowners considering an easement or donation, a site visit helps us understand the property's conservation values. It's also a chance for you to ask questions face-to-face.

4. **We explore options together.** Based on your goals, we'll explain which tools might be a good fit—and which might not. There's no one-size-fits-all answer.

5. **You decide.** If you want to move forward, we guide you through the process step by step. If you need time, take it. There's no rush and no pressure.

**This is your decision, your timeline, and your land.** We're here to help—not to push.

[block:faq]
title: Questions Landowners Ask

**Will I lose control of my land?**
No. With a conservation easement, you keep full ownership. You decide what restrictions make sense. You can still live on the land, farm it, sell it, or leave it to your heirs. We just ensure that certain protections—the ones *you* agreed to—stay in place forever.

**Does the government get involved?**
No. Conservation easements are private, voluntary agreements between you and the Door County Land Trust. No government agency is involved in the decision or the agreement. (Tax benefits, if you pursue them, are processed through your own tax filings—like any charitable donation.)

**What if my kids want to build a house someday?**
Easements are flexible. If it's important to you, we can reserve a building envelope or homesite for future use. We'll work with you to balance protection with your family's future needs.

**What happens if I sell the land?**
The easement stays with the property. New owners must follow the same restrictions. That's the point—it's permanent protection, regardless of who owns the land next.

**What does it cost me?**
Nothing. The Land Trust covers the cost of drafting easements, ecological assessments, legal filings, and ongoing stewardship. You may have your own legal or tax advisor review documents, but our services are free.

**Can I change my mind later?**
Conservation easements are permanent by design—that's what makes them meaningful. Once recorded, they cannot be undone. That's why we take time upfront to make sure this is the right decision for you.

**What about property taxes?**
It depends. Some landowners see reduced property taxes after an easement because the land's development value has been removed. Others see little change. We can't guarantee tax outcomes, but we can help you understand the possibilities.

**How long does the process take?**
It varies. A straightforward easement might take six months; more complex situations can take a year or more. There's no set timeline—we move at your pace.

**What if I'm not sure yet?**
That's fine. Most landowners take months or years to decide. You can reach out just to learn more—no commitment required. We're happy to answer questions whenever you're ready.
[/block:faq]
