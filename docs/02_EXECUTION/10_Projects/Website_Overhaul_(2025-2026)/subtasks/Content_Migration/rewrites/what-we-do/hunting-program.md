# PROGRAM INTRO

## Hunters as Conservation Partners

Hunters play an important role in maintaining healthy ecosystems on Land Trust preserves. White-tailed deer and wild turkeys, when overabundant, damage native plants and forest regeneration. By helping manage these populations, hunters contribute directly to our conservation mission.

Most Land Trust-owned preserves are open to hunting during Wisconsin DNR seasons. A few small properties are closed due to size, location, or ecological sensitivity.

**Important:** Lands under conservation easement are private property. The Land Trust cannot grant hunting permission on easement lands — you must contact the landowner directly.

### How to Use This Guide

Each preserve has **color-coded zones** shown on its hunting map:

**To find where you can hunt:**
1. Choose a preserve from the list below
2. Download the hunting map (PDF)
3. Note which color zones apply to your planned hunt

# RULES

## Hunting Rules

Follow these rules on all Land Trust preserves. Failure to comply may result in loss of hunting privileges and referral to WI-DNR or Door County Sheriff.

### Before You Hunt

1. **Register with the Land Trust** — Complete the [Hunter Information Form](#register) before your first hunt of the season
2. **Know the property** — Download and review the hunting map for your preserve
3. **Check the zones** — Confirm your planned hunt is permitted in that color zone
4. **Follow all WI-DNR regulations** — Season dates, licensing, and bag limits apply

### Equipment Rules

| Allowed | Not Allowed |
|---------|-------------|
| Portable stands (day use only) | Permanent stands or blinds |
| Trail cameras (day use only) | Overnight stands or cameras |
| Stands with owner ID (name, address, phone) | Stands without identification |
| Dogs off-leash while actively hunting* | Motorized vehicles of any kind |

*Dogs must be leashed April 15 – July 31 to protect ground-nesting birds.

### Prohibited Activities

- **No baiting** — Bait piles are not permitted
- **No cutting vegetation** — Do not cut trees, branches, or brush
- **No overnight structures** — All stands and cameras must be removed each night
- **No motorized vehicles** — ATVs, UTVs, and vehicles are prohibited on preserves

### Respect the Land and Neighbors

- Stay within preserve boundaries
- If tracking a wounded animal onto private land, get landowner permission first
- Report any suspicious activity to the Land Trust
- Pack out everything you bring in

### Can I leave my tree stand overnight?

**No.** All portable stands, blinds, and trail cameras must be removed at the end of each day. Stands left overnight will be removed by staff. Make sure your stand has your name, address, and phone number on it.

---

### Do I need permission to hunt on Land Trust preserves?

You don't need individual permission, but we ask that you **register before hunting** by completing our [Hunter Information Form](#register). This helps us understand preserve usage and contact hunters if needed.

---

### Can I hunt on conservation easement lands?

**No — not through us.** Easement lands are private property. The Land Trust cannot grant hunting permission. You must contact the landowner directly.

---

### What's the difference between the color zones?

Each preserve map shows colored zones with different rules:
- **Orange:** All seasons and methods permitted
- **Green:** Most seasons, some restrictions (check table)
- **Blue:** Archery and crossbow only
- **Red:** No hunting

Always download the map for your preserve and check which zones apply.

---

### Can I use bait?

**No.** Baiting is not permitted on any Land Trust preserve.

---

### Can I bring my dog?

**Yes, while actively hunting** — dogs engaged in hunting may be off-leash. However, all dogs must be leashed **April 15 – July 31** to protect ground-nesting birds.

---

### What about trapping?

Contact us at hunting@doorcountylandtrust.org for information about trapping regulations on specific preserves.

# HIKER SAFETY

## Tips for Hikers During Hunting Season

Our preserves are open to everyone year-round, including during hunting seasons. Hunters and hikers share these spaces safely by staying aware.

**If you're hiking during hunting season:**

- **Wear blaze orange or bright colors** — Visibility protects you
- **Stay on marked trails** — Hunters expect hikers on trails, not off-trail
- **Make noise** — Talk, whistle, or use bells so hunters know you're nearby
- **Keep pets leashed** — Especially important during hunting season
- **Avoid white clothing** — It resembles the tail of a white-tailed deer
- **Check which preserves are closed to hunting** — These are always safe for quiet walks

**[View WI-DNR hunting season dates](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/)** to know when seasons are active.
